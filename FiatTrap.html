The Machine

Where Money Actually Comes From

Most people assume money is printed by governments and distributed into the economy. The reality is far stranger — and far more consequential. The vast majority of money in circulation today was created out of nothing by commercial banks, and is backed by nothing except the promise to repay debt.

When you take out a mortgage for $400,000, your bank does not lend you money it already has in a vault. It types $400,000 into a computer, credits your account, and simultaneously creates a $400,000 liability on its books. The money did not exist before that keystroke. This is called fractional reserve banking, and it is the foundation of the entire modern financial system.

Layer on top of this the central bank — the Federal Reserve — which creates base money through "quantitative easing": purchasing government bonds and mortgage-backed securities by crediting bank reserves it conjures from thin air. The money supply expands with every crisis, every recession, every war, every election cycle where spending promises must be kept.

In 2020 alone, the US Federal Reserve expanded its balance sheet by $3 trillion in eight weeks.
The same amount took 100 years to accumulate between 1913 and 2008.

The Hidden Tax

Inflation Is Not a Natural Disaster. It Is a Policy Choice.

When the government needs money it doesn't have — for wars, bailouts, stimulus programs, or simply to cover the gap between what it promises and what it collects in taxes — it has a powerful option unavailable to ordinary citizens: it can create the money. Or it can instruct the central bank to buy its debt with newly created reserves, which amounts to the same thing.

The result is inflation. Prices rise because more money is chasing the same amount of goods. Your grocery bill goes up. Your rent goes up. Your savings, sitting in a bank account earning 0.5%, lose purchasing power in real terms every year. You were never asked to pay this tax.
