The Scaling Problem

Bitcoin Is the Perfect Store of Value. It Was Never Meant to Buy Coffee.

The Bitcoin base layer processes roughly 7 transactions per second. Visa handles 24,000. For Bitcoin to become a global payment rail — used by billions of people for daily purchases, business invoices, and international remittances — something had to change. But changing the base layer meant risking the very security and decentralization that make Bitcoin worth anything in the first place.

The answer was not to change Bitcoin. The answer was to build on top of it.

Layer 2 protocols let users transact with Bitcoin's security guarantees without writing every payment to the blockchain. Think of it like a tab at a bar — you run up the tab all night, but only one transaction settles at the end. Except with Lightning, the tab settles in milliseconds and can route payments across the entire planet through a trustless network of payment channels.

Bitcoin base layer: ~7 TPS · $1–$5 per transaction · 10-minute confirmation
Lightning Network: 1,000,000+ TPS theoretical · <$0.01 · <1 second settlement

How It Works

Payment Channels, HTLCs, and the Routing Miracle

Lightning works through a network of bidirectional payment channels. Two parties lock Bitcoin into a shared 2-of-2 multisignature address on-chain — this "opens" the channel. From that point, they can transact back and forth as many times as they want, instantly and for free, without touching the blockchain. Only when they choose to "close" the channel is the final balance settled on-chain.

The real magic is routing. You don't need a direct channel with every person you want to pay. Payments route through a web of existing channels using Hashed Timelock Contracts — cryptographic agreements that guarantee the payment either completes fully or fails completely. There is no partial payment, no trust required, no intermediary holding your money.

The Liquid Network

Bitcoin's Confidential Settlement Layer

While Lightning optimizes for speed and micropayments, the Liquid Network — built by Blockstream — solves a different problem: confidential, fast settlement for institutions, traders, and users who need privacy and asset issuance capabilities.

Liquid uses a federated sidechain model. Users "peg in" real Bitcoin to receive L-BTC at a 1:1 ratio, transact on the sidechain with 2-minute finality and confidential transaction amounts, then "peg out" back to Bitcoin whenever they choose. Critically, Liquid supports native stablecoins — Tether's USDt runs on Liquid, meaning users in countries with volatile currencies can hold dollar-denominated assets backed by Bitcoin infrastructure.

AQUA The wallet by JAN3 (Samson Mow) that unifies Bitcoin, Lightning, and Liquid in one interface. Hold BTC, send Lightning payments, and hold USDt on Liquid — all from one app. Pay network fees in any asset, no separate "gas" token required. Financial freedom for the 4 billion unbanked.

Aqua is designed specifically for emerging markets — countries where local currencies collapse regularly, where banking infrastructure is unreliable, and where people need dollar-stable savings alongside Bitcoin exposure. In Argentina, Zimbabwe, or Lebanon, Aqua gives users a way to escape currency destruction without depending on a bank at all.
