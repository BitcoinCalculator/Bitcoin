The Logic

Why Nation-States Are Forced to Play

Bitcoin's fixed supply of 21 million coins creates a strategic dynamic unlike anything in the history of monetary policy. Once one nation-state begins accumulating Bitcoin as a reserve asset, every other nation faces a stark choice: acquire Bitcoin now, at today's price, or acquire it later — at whatever price sovereign demand has pushed it to.

This is not a new concept in geopolitics. Nations race to acquire oil, rare earth minerals, enriched uranium — any strategic resource with limited supply and rising demand. Bitcoin simply compresses this dynamic into a 21-million-unit hard cap that no government, no central bank, and no act of legislation can change.

The first mover advantage is profound. El Salvador, a country with a GDP smaller than many American cities, now holds a sovereign Bitcoin treasury that has appreciated dramatically from its average cost basis. Bhutan's hydropower-funded mining operation quietly accumulated thousands of coins before the world noticed. And in March 2025, the United States federal government — the issuer of the world's reserve currency — officially designated Bitcoin a strategic reserve asset.

When the US — the world's largest economy and issuer of the global reserve currency — formally adopts Bitcoin as a strategic reserve asset, every other nation's treasury department must ask the same question: should we?
Game Theory

The Prisoner's Dilemma at Sovereign Scale

The game theory of nation-state Bitcoin adoption follows a classic defection dynamic. The optimal individual strategy conflicts with collective inaction, driving each actor toward accumulation regardless of what others do.

01
The First Mover Gains the Most
Nations that accumulate Bitcoin early acquire coins at lower prices, before sovereign demand is priced in. Their cost basis is far lower than any late entrant. El Salvador began buying in 2021 at an average around $43,000 per coin — a price that may look extraordinarily cheap in a decade.
02
Waiting Is a Losing Strategy
For every month a nation delays, competing nations accumulate more of the fixed supply at lower prices. The pool of available coins shrinks as long-term holders — including sovereign funds — remove liquidity from exchanges. Demand rises; supply cannot increase. Price discovery is ruthless.
03
The Dollar's Reserve Status Is Not Permanent
History shows that reserve currencies rotate. The British pound, the Dutch guilder, the Spanish real — each dominated global trade before yielding to the next monetary hegemon. Nations hedging against dollar depreciation, sanctions exposure, and currency wars increasingly view Bitcoin as the only truly neutral reserve asset with no counterparty risk and no single issuer.
04
Mining Is Sovereign Minting
Nations with cheap energy — hydropower in Bhutan and Paraguay, geothermal in El Salvador and Iceland, stranded natural gas in the US — can mine Bitcoin directly, converting otherwise wasted energy into sovereign digital capital. This is arguably the most capital-efficient monetary strategy available to energy-rich nations in the 21st century.
05
Bitcoin Is Immune to Sanctions
Nations holding dollar-denominated reserves discovered in 2022 that the United States can freeze those assets instantly — as Russia learned when $300 billion in sovereign reserves were immobilized. Bitcoin held in sovereign cold storage answers to no government, no SWIFT network, no bank. This is not a theoretical advantage; it is an immediate geopolitical reality for dozens of nations.

The First Mover

What El Salvador Actually Did

On June 9, 2021, El Salvador's Legislative Assembly passed the Bitcoin Law with 62 of 84 votes — the first act of sovereign recognition for Bitcoin as legal tender anywhere in the world. President Nayib Bukele, then 39, had announced the bill just three days earlier at a Miami Bitcoin conference to a standing ovation.

The geopolitical context matters. El Salvador is a country where 70% of the population had no bank account and over $6 billion per year flows in from Salvadorans working abroad — most of it bleeding 10–20% in Western Union and MoneyGram fees. Bitcoin on Lightning offered a direct alternative: near-instant international transfers for fractions of a cent, available to anyone with a smartphone.

The government deployed the Chivo wallet — a state-sponsored Bitcoin and dollar wallet — and seeded it with $30 in Bitcoin for every Salvadoran who signed up. Within weeks, more Salvadorans had downloaded Chivo than had bank accounts. The IMF protested. Fitch downgraded El Salvador's debt. The country held anyway.

~$500M Estimated value of El Salvador's Bitcoin treasury at May 2025 prices — acquired at an average cost basis near $43,000. The nation turned a sovereign bet into a sovereign gain, and inspired a global conversation that no amount of IMF pressure has silenced.

In early 2025, under IMF loan conditions, El Salvador amended its Bitcoin law to make merchant acceptance voluntary rather than mandatory. This was widely reported as a retreat — but the government retained every Bitcoin in its treasury and has continued accumulating. The Bitcoin Office remains active and the country's Bitcoin-focused infrastructure investment continues.

The Silent Miner

How Bhutan Built a Digital Treasury in Secret

In April 2023, Forbes revealed that the Royal Government of Bhutan — population 800,000, landlocked in the Himalayas — had been mining Bitcoin for four years through Druk Holdings and Investments, the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund. The disclosure was a revelation: a tiny nation had executed one of the most sophisticated sovereign Bitcoin strategies on the planet, in complete silence.

Bhutan's advantage is energy. The country generates far more hydroelectric power from its glacial rivers than it can use domestically, historically exporting the surplus to India. Bitcoin mining gave Bhutan a new buyer for that energy — one that converts kilowatt-hours directly into digital assets sitting in sovereign cold storage. No export infrastructure needed. No dependence on a single customer. Pure monetary sovereignty minted from mountain water.

Bhutan's ~6,000 BTC represents roughly 25% of the country's annual GDP in stored value — an extraordinary concentration of national wealth in a single digital asset, accumulated at near-zero marginal cost by deploying surplus renewable energy.

Bhutan has partnered with several Western mining firms to expand capacity, and geopolitically, its approach offers a template for any energy-surplus nation: convert stranded power into digital capital that requires no physical storage, no shipping, and cannot be confiscated by any foreign power. Paraguay, Laos, and Iceland are watching closely.

The Superpower Moves

The US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve

On March 6, 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order establishing the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve — directing the US Treasury and Department of Justice to consolidate all Bitcoin held by the federal government into a single sovereign reserve, and prohibiting its sale. The order also directed relevant agencies to develop strategies for acquiring additional Bitcoin in a budget-neutral manner.

The order was simultaneously symbolic and structural. Symbolically, it ended the federal government's practice of auctioning seized Bitcoin — a policy that saw the US Treasury repeatedly sell coins (often near local price lows) that would have been worth multiples of their sale price just months later. Structurally, it created a new category of government asset: a digital reserve held not as evidence or forfeiture, but as national capital.

"The United States will not sell its Bitcoin. It will hold it as a strategic reserve asset, just as it holds gold."

— Implied framework of EO 14178, March 2025
200,000+ BTC Estimated US federal Bitcoin holdings. At $100,000 per coin, this represents $20 billion in digital sovereign capital — roughly the same value as the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve at its peak. The US government is, by a wide margin, the largest known sovereign holder of Bitcoin on Earth.

The strategic implications extend beyond the balance sheet. When the United States — the country that issues the world's reserve currency — designates Bitcoin as a strategic reserve asset, it sends an unmistakable signal to every other nation's treasury department, central bank, and sovereign wealth fund. The asset has been legitimized at the highest possible level of financial authority.

Some analysts point to a deeper irony: the United States, whose Federal Reserve helped motivate the creation of Bitcoin in the first place, is now one of its largest holders. The government that printed $3 trillion in eight weeks in 2020 now holds digital assets whose supply cannot be inflated by any entity on Earth.

The Mathematics

21 Million Coins. 195 Countries. Do the Math.

There are approximately 19.7 million Bitcoin in circulation as of mid-2025. Roughly 3–4 million are estimated to be permanently lost — sent to inaccessible wallets, lost hard drives, forgotten seed phrases from the early years. The effective circulating supply available for purchase is therefore somewhere between 15 and 16 million Bitcoin.

Divide that among 195 countries. The average nation-state can acquire roughly 80,000 Bitcoin if the distribution were perfectly equal — which it will not be. The first movers take the most. The laggards get priced out. Small nations that delay may find themselves unable to build meaningful reserves at any price they can politically sustain.

The Geopolitics

Bitcoin as Neutral Reserve: The End of Dollar Dominance?

The United States dollar achieved its reserve currency status through a combination of factors: the Bretton Woods agreement, US military and economic dominance, the petrodollar system anchored by the 1974 Saudi Arabia deal, and the sheer depth of US Treasury markets. For 80 years, holding dollars meant holding stability.

But cracks have appeared. The 2022 freezing of Russian sovereign reserves demonstrated that dollar-denominated assets are ultimately subject to US political discretion. Nations that had long assumed their dollar holdings were inviolable discovered otherwise in weeks. The search for genuinely neutral reserve assets accelerated dramatically — and Bitcoin, with its permissionless architecture, no issuing sovereign, and immunity to unilateral seizure, entered central bank conversations that would have seemed absurd five years earlier.

The shift is not ideological — it is actuarial. Nations diversifying reserves into Bitcoin are not making a political statement about fiat currency. They are managing sovereign risk. Just as a prudent investor holds gold alongside equities, a prudent nation in a multipolar world holds some Bitcoin alongside its dollar and euro reserves. The question is no longer whether — it is how much, and when.

"Bitcoin is the first money in history that no government issued, no army backs, and no border stops. That is not a bug. For billions of people living under financial repression, it is the only feature that matters."

— Paraphrasing the thesis shared across sovereign Bitcoin adopters
